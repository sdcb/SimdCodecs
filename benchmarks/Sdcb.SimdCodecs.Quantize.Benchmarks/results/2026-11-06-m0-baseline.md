# M0 baseline, 2026-11-06

The repository's quantizer before the rewrite (Wu's 32³ RGB cube, `src/Shared/PaletteQuantizer.cs` at 91bf9e0, as `ours-wu`) against pngquant and ImageSharp. Recorded before any quantizer change, to fix where we start.

- Machine: AMD Ryzen 7 5800X (8 cores / 16 threads), Windows 11 10.0.26200, .NET 10.0.11, x64, Vector256 accelerated, Vector512 not.
- pngquant 2.17.0 (September 2021), `--speed 4`, `--nofs` when not dithering; black-box CLI, time includes process start-up and PNG I/O.
- ImageSharp 3.1.12.
- Command: `dotnet run -c Release --project benchmarks/Sdcb.SimdCodecs.Quantize.Benchmarks -- rd` (photos at 1024 px long side, colours 256/128/64/32/16, dither off and on).

## Findings

- Without dithering the old Wu quantizer is within a few percent of pngquant on size at equal SSIM (+2.8%), but loses badly where colours crowd into a few bins of its 5-bit grid: `photo-night-brussels` has 224 distinct colours, which pngquant keeps exactly, and the 32³ cube merges into 30 (SSIM 13.6 dB against lossless).
- It has no alpha other than a 1-bit threshold: on `sticker` its OKLab error stays at 5.6 whatever the palette size (pngquant: 0.8 at 256 colours).
- With dithering every contestant falls far behind pngquant (+40% and worse): plain Floyd–Steinberg noise costs both SSIM and deflate.
- Time: ours is 7–10% of pngquant's process time, ImageSharp Wu 14–20%.
